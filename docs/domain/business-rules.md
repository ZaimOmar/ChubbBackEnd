# Business rules

Label: **(industry-typical)** = common claims practice; **(assumption)** = our choice, not Chubb policy. Each rule: statement, parameters, rationale, test. Focus is the claim progress trail; the rest is kept minimal.

## A. Lifecycle
- **BR-01** (industry-typical, revised) Statuses: Submitted, UnderReview, InfoRequested, PendingDecisionApproval, Approved, Rejected, PendingSettlementApproval, Settling, Settled. Allowed transitions only: Submitted>UnderReview, UnderReview>InfoRequested, InfoRequested>UnderReview, UnderReview>PendingDecisionApproval (officer proposes approve or reject), PendingDecisionApproval>Approved or >Rejected (manager confirms, matching the proposal), PendingDecisionApproval>UnderReview (send-back or withdrawal), Approved>PendingSettlementApproval (officer requests settlement), PendingSettlementApproval>Settling (manager confirms; payable frozen and payment instruction created, BR-41/BR-44), Settling>Settled (only on the PaymentCompleted event, actor System, BR-48), Settling>Approved (manager cancels the payment, BR-50), PendingSettlementApproval>Approved (send-back or withdrawal). Rejected and Settled are final. `Settling` means the payment has been dispatched or is being retried; a payment failure after retries leaves the claim in Settling. There is no PendingSettlementApproval>Settled transition any more. Why two waiting states plus Settling and not more: each money-affecting step needs one waiting state; one shared "Pending" state would hide which step is awaited and break queues and metrics; the proposed outcome (approve/reject) is stored on the proposal, not as extra states. Test: every other pair returns 409/422 and writes no state change.
- **BR-02** (assumption, revised) Who acts: Claimant submits and answers information requests. Assigned officer does Submitted>UnderReview, info requests, proposals (decision and settlement request) and withdrawals. Manager (as checker) does the confirmations Pending*>Approved/Rejected/Settling and send-backs, reassignment, and the payment actions of BR-49/BR-50 (retry, correct payee, cancel) and the returned-payment queue (BR-52). Manager never proposes. Claimant also supplies payee bank details after approval (BR-42). System (payment service) moves Settling>Settled. Role matrix for the new areas: ledger view (BR-62) and pool fund view and top-up (BR-69): Manager only, Officer and Claimant get 403; payment retry, payee correction and cancel: Manager only, Officer gets 403; payee details submit: the claim's claimant only. Test: wrong role gets 403; non-assigned officer gets 403 on propose/withdraw; officer gets 403 on confirm.
- **BR-78** (industry-typical principle: segregation of duties and conflict of interest; assumption on mechanics) A user account that holds the Claimant role never also holds a staff role (Officer or Manager); the combination is refused when the user is created or changed. Officer plus Manager on one account is allowed on purpose (BR-32). A staff member who is also a policyholder (e.g. an employee with motor or home cover) claims through a separate claimant account; that staff account must not handle or check claims on their own policies, a conflict-of-interest control that is documented, not built (no link between the two identities exists in this system). Rationale: claimant rules (own claims only, Public entries only, BR-12, BR-08/BR-09) and staff rules (all claims, Internal entries, maker or checker) cannot both apply to one token without one of them leaking: a combined account would see the Internal trail of its own claim and could pick up, assess or confirm it. Test: creating a user with Claimant plus Officer, or Claimant plus Manager, is rejected; Officer plus Manager succeeds; no seeded user holds Claimant with any other role.
- **BR-03** (assumption) Final claims are never reopened, withdrawn or edited (documented, not built). A bank-returned payment does not change this: the claim stays Settled and the unpaid money is carried by the reinstated payable (BR-52); reopening a claim for payment only is documented, not built. Test: any command on Rejected/Settled returns 409.

## B. Claim progress trail
- **BR-04** (industry-typical, revised) Each of these creates exactly one entry: submitted, assigned, reassigned, info requested, info answered, assessed amount set or changed, decision proposed, decision confirmed (approved or rejected), decision sent back, settlement requested, settlement confirmed (claim moves to Settling), payee details provided, payment dispatched, payment completed (claim Settled), payment attempt failed, payment retries exhausted, manager retry, payee correction or cancel, payment status check with unknown outcome, payment reversed (bank return), payment cancelled, settlement sent back, proposal withdrawn, correction issued (BR-74), and every refused attempt by an authenticated user (invalid transition, unauthorised action, maker=checker attempt, lost concurrent confirm) as an Internal entry. Test: after each command, accepted or refused, entry count rises by exactly 1. A refused attempt writes the entry and nothing else (no state change); unauthenticated calls write nothing.
- **BR-05** (industry-typical) Mandatory fields: id, claimId, occurredAt (server UTC), eventType, actorId, actorRole, summary, visibility, correlationId; fromStatus/toStatus whenever status changes (fromStatus null on submission); reason where a rule below requires it; for proposal-related entries also makerId and, on confirm/send-back, checkerId, plus proposalId linking the entries of one proposal. Test: entry missing a mandatory field is refused.
- **BR-06** (industry-typical) Immutable: no update or delete. A correction is a new entry of type `Correction` that references the earlier entry id and states why; who may issue it, when and its visibility are in BR-74. Why: audit and dispute evidence. Test: update/delete attempts fail; correction leaves the original unchanged.
- **BR-07** (industry-typical) Entry and state change are written in one transaction; neither exists without the other. Test: forced failure leaves both unchanged.
- **BR-08** (assumption) Public entries (claimant may see), with example summaries: "Claim received: CLM-SG-2026-000123" (BR-75); "Claim picked up for review"; "Decision under review" (on decision proposal); "Settlement under review" (on settlement request); "Decision under review" is also shown again after a decision send-back, with no detail; "Information requested: please send the police report"; "Information received"; "Claim approved for SGD 8,000" and "Claim rejected: Not covered, policy excludes flood damage" (only when the checker confirms); "Claim approved: please provide your bank details" (after approval, BR-42); "Payment on the way" (payment dispatched); "Claim settled: SGD 7,500 paid, reference PAY-..." (only on PaymentCompleted, never on manager confirmation). The proposal itself, the proposed outcome, the maker and checker identities, send-back notes and withdrawals are never Public. See BR-33. Test: claimant timeline contains only these types.
- **BR-09** (assumption) Internal entries (claimant must never see, including count or gaps): "Assessed amount set to 7,500, estimate looked high"; "Reassigned from Officer A to Officer B, workload"; "Approved above estimate: second quote supplied"; "Reserve adjusted 9,000 to 7,500"; "Refused transition attempt: settle on UnderReview"; "Decision proposed by Officer A: approve 7,500, reason: repair quote verified"; "Decision sent back by Manager M: quote not attached, please attach"; "Refused: Officer A tried to confirm own proposal"; "Refused: confirm lost to concurrent confirm by Manager N"; "Proposal withdrawn by Officer A: customer supplied new document"; "Payment attempt 2 failed: bank timeout"; "Payment retries exhausted, sent to manager queue"; "Payee details corrected by Manager M: account number typo". Payment failures are never Public. Test: claimant API response has no Internal entry and no field revealing one.
- **BR-10** (industry-typical) Reason captured for: reject proposal (code + text), approve above estimate, reassignment (mandatory), correction (mandatory, min 10 characters, BR-74), info request (the question), send-back (mandatory), withdrawal (mandatory), decision proposal (short rationale, min 10 characters, assumption). Test: missing reason returns validation error.
- **BR-11** (industry-typical) Actor and role recorded are taken from the token, never from the request body; system actions use role `System`. Test: spoofed actor in body is ignored.
- **BR-12** (industry-typical) Claimant sees own claim only; officer and manager see Public + Internal for any claim in their view (a manager sees all claims, including the pending checker queue). Entries are ordered by occurredAt then id.

## C. Information requests
- **BR-13** (industry-typical) Only the assigned officer, only from UnderReview, with a non-empty question. One open request at a time. Test: second open request returns 409.
- **BR-14** (industry-typical) Claimant sees the question, the date and the due date; not the officer's internal notes. Public entry on raise.
- **BR-15** (industry-typical) Info requests can only be raised from UnderReview, never while a proposal is pending. A response from the claim's claimant returns the claim to UnderReview with a Public "Information received" entry; the same officer keeps the claim. Test: response on a claim not InfoRequested returns 409.
- **BR-16** (assumption) Due date = 14 days after request. Optional: a system Internal entry "Reminder due" on day 14; no auto-reject. Test (if built): reminder created once.

## D. Decisions
- **BR-17** (industry-typical, revised) A reject proposal needs a reason code and free text (min 10 characters, assumption), recorded on the proposal and applied only on confirmation. Codes (accepted by the human): `NOT_COVERED`, `POLICY_INACTIVE`, `EXCLUSION_APPLIES`, `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`, `DUPLICATE_CLAIM`, `NO_RESPONSE`. Test: unknown code or empty text returns 422.
- **BR-18** (industry-typical, revised) Proposed approved amount (net payable) > 0 and cannot exceed the estimated amount unless the officer records a justification (Internal entry on the proposal). Test: above-estimate without justification returns 422.
- **BR-19** (industry-typical, revised) Amounts are net of excess: payable = max(assessed amount minus policy excess, 0), then capped at the remaining coverage limit (BR-72: coverage limit minus paid-to-date minus frozen unpaid payables of other claims on the same policy; assumption: the limit is an aggregate per policy period, a per-claim or per-event limit is documented, not built; seeded policies carry no prior payments, so remaining = limit in the demo). The approved amount equals payable; a payable of 0 cannot be approved (reject instead). Excess is a seeded policy attribute in the claim currency. Rationale: the insurer pays the loss less the policyholder's share, within the limit. Test: assessed 10,000, excess 500, limit 8,000 gives payable 8,000; assessed 400, excess 500 gives 0 and approve returns 422.
- **BR-20** (industry-typical, revised) Only an Approved claim with payee details on file (BR-42) can have settlement requested; only a PendingSettlementApproval claim can be confirmed (by the checker), which moves it to Settling and creates the payment (BR-44); the claim becomes Settled only when PaymentCompleted is received (BR-48). Settlement pays exactly the payable of BR-41, once. Test: request on any other status returns 409; request without payee details returns 422; confirm twice returns 409 and creates one payment; the claim is not Settled after confirm.
- **BR-21** (assumption) A decision proposal requires an assessed amount to exist (set during review, Internal entry). Test: propose approve without assessed amount returns 422.

## E. Reserve
- **BR-22** (industry-typical, simplified, revised) Reserve rules: on submit reserve = estimated amount; on assessment reserve = net payable of the assessed amount (BR-19); while a decision or settlement proposal is pending reserve is UNCHANGED; on confirmed approve reserve = approved amount; on confirmed reject reserve = 0 (released); on confirmed settlement (claim to Settling) reserve = 0 because the amount moves into the frozen payable (BR-41, ledger BR-55); paid = approved amount is recorded only at PaymentCompleted (claim Settled); on payment cancel (BR-50) reserve is restored to the payable amount; on send-back or withdrawal nothing changes. Exposure, with the Settling addition: liability exposure stays the outstanding reserve on open claims (Settling claims are open, reserve 0), and a separate figure "in settlement" = payable not yet paid on Settling claims is shown beside it so money owed is never invisible (BR-61). Each change is recorded Internal. Rationale: money moves only on the second signature, so exposure never reflects an unconfirmed proposal. Test: exposure sums reserve of non-final claims only (incl. Settling at 0) (Pending* claims included at their current reserve); incurred = paid + reserve; proposing, sending back and withdrawing leave reserve, paid and exposure identical.

## F. Market and currency
- **BR-23** (assumption) Markets and currency: SG SGD, HK HKD, MY MYR, TH THB, ID IDR, AU AUD. A claim's currency is fixed by its policy's market and never changes. Amounts are stored as decimal(18,2) with the currency code. Test: claim currency differing from market currency is refused.
- **BR-24** (assumption) USD is for reporting only, converted with a static, dated table (illustrative, not market rates): SGD 0.74, HKD 0.128, MYR 0.22, THB 0.029, IDR 0.000062, AUD 0.65. Conversion never alters stored amounts. Test: exposure USD = sum(reserve x rate).

## G. Claim number
- **BR-25** (assumption) Format `CLM-{MKT}-{YYYY}-{NNNNNN}`, e.g. `CLM-SG-2026-000123`; sequence per market and year; unique; never reused. Test: format regex and uniqueness under concurrent submits.

## H. Pick-up
- **BR-26** (industry-typical) One officer per claim. Only a Submitted claim can be picked up; if already assigned the second attempt gets 409. Test: two simultaneous pick-ups, exactly one wins.
- **BR-27** (assumption, settled) A manager may reassign a claim while UnderReview, InfoRequested, or in a Pending* status (see BR-37 for pending); the current officer may also release it. Reassignment requires a reason, creates an Internal trail entry (from, to, reason, who), status unchanged. Test: entry present, assignee changed, missing reason returns 422.

## I. Metrics (derived from the trail)
- **BR-28** (industry-typical, revised) Open per officer = claims assigned and not Rejected/Settled (Pending* and Settling included). Ageing = now minus submission time, in days, for open claims. Outstanding count and oldest unassigned follow BR-76 and BR-77. Pending checker queue = claims in PendingDecisionApproval or PendingSettlementApproval, oldest proposal first, with count and age per manager view; shown separately from the officer's own working claims (an officer's pending claims are "waiting on checker").
- **BR-29** (assumption, settled) Handling time = first Submitted entry to the final decision, i.e. the confirmed Approved or Rejected entry. It therefore includes the time spent pending decision approval. Settlement and payment are excluded: settlement time (confirmed Approved entry to the Settled entry, i.e. PaymentCompleted, including pending settlement approval and Settling) is a separate metric (BR-51); payment time (Settling entry to Settled entry) is reported inside it. Manager queues for failed payments are not part of handling time. Average over decided claims per officer.
- **BR-30** Time in status = gap between consecutive status-changing entries; the open status runs to now. Pending statuses and Settling are statuses like any other. Test: sum of time in status from Submitted to the confirmed decision equals handling time.
- **BR-40** (assumption) Checker turnaround time = proposal entry to the confirm or send-back entry of that proposal (matched by proposalId); reported per manager and overall, for decisions and settlements separately. Send-backs are counted and reported as a send-back rate per officer (proposals sent back / proposals made). Withdrawals are excluded from turnaround. Test: turnaround equals the timestamp gap of the two linked entries.

## J. Maker-checker (four-eyes)
- **BR-31** (industry-typical) Scope: the approve/reject decision and the settlement. The maker is the assigned officer; the checker is a user holding the Manager role (an Officer-only user can never check). Nothing else (info requests, assessment, pick-up) needs a checker. Rationale: separation of duties over money-affecting acts, standard fraud and error control. Test: confirm by an Officer-only user returns 403.
- **BR-32** (industry-typical) The maker can never be the checker of their own proposal, even if the same user holds both Officer and Manager roles; compared on user id. The attempt returns 403, and a refused Internal entry is written (BR-04). Test: user with both roles proposes then confirms, 403.
- **BR-33** (assumption) Visibility. Public: "Decision under review" / "Settlement under review" while pending (no outcome, amount, reason code, names), and the final confirmed approve, reject (with code and text) or settled entry. Internal: proposal content, proposed outcome and amount, rationale, makerId, checkerId, send-back reasons and notes, withdrawals, refused attempts. After a send-back the claimant continues to see "Decision under review" and nothing else; the claimant is never told a send-back happened. Test: claimant timeline during and after a send-back contains no proposed outcome, no send-back, no staff identity.
- **BR-34** (industry-typical) A checker may confirm (applies the proposal exactly as made) or send back with a mandatory reason (min 10 characters, assumption). The checker cannot alter amount, code or outcome; changing it means send-back. Test: confirm has no editable payload; send-back without reason returns 422.
- **BR-35** (industry-typical) While a proposal is pending, no reserve, paid or exposure change occurs (see BR-22). Test: values identical before proposal and after send-back.
- **BR-36** (industry-typical) A pending proposal is immutable: it cannot be edited, no new info request, assessment change or second proposal can be made on the claim. The maker may only withdraw it (reason required, Internal entry) or the checker send it back; both return the claim to UnderReview (decision) or Approved (settlement). To change a proposal the maker withdraws and re-proposes. Test: edit, assess or propose on a Pending* claim returns 409.
- **BR-37** (assumption) If the maker is reassigned while a proposal is pending, the proposal is automatically withdrawn: the claim returns to UnderReview or Approved, an Internal entry records reassignment and auto-withdrawal, and the new officer becomes maker of any new proposal. Why: the proposal carries the original officer's accountability; the new owner must form their own view. BR-32 applies per proposal: the former maker may check a later proposal made by someone else. Test: reassign on Pending* claim leaves status UnderReview/Approved and assignee changed, no pending proposal remains.
- **BR-38** (industry-typical) Concurrent confirms: exactly one wins. The first valid confirm or send-back moves the claim; any other attempt on the same proposal gets 409 and a refused Internal entry. Same for confirm racing against withdrawal. Test: two simultaneous confirms, one succeeds, one 409, one transition entry only; money applied once.
- **BR-39** (industry-typical) Every proposal and its outcome is traceable: all entries of one proposal share a proposalId, and the confirm/send-back entry names both maker and checker. A claim may have several proposals over its life (after send-backs); only one is open at a time. Test: confirmed decision entry has makerId, checkerId, proposalId, and makerId != checkerId.

## K. Payment (module build; money movement)
Payments are made by a separate Payment service. Nothing here lets any person key a payment directly; a payment exists only because a manager confirmed a settlement.
- **BR-41** (industry-typical, assumption on rounding) Payable. Payable = assessed amount minus policy excess, floored at 0, capped at the remaining coverage limit (same as BR-19). It is computed once, when the decision is approved, and frozen on a payable record (claimId, amount, currency, computedAt, inputs: assessed, excess, limit). It never recalculates; a different amount needs a send-back/new proposal before approval, or a cancel after (BR-50). Test: change the policy excess after approval, payable unchanged; assessed 8,000, excess 500 gives 7,500.
- **BR-42** (industry-typical, assumption on masking and encryption) Payee details are captured from the claimant AFTER approval, not at intake. On approval the claimant gets an alert (BR-64) and submits payee type (BR-43), payee name, bank name, account number and bank code (SWIFT/BIC or local routing code). Only the claim's claimant may submit; the officer cannot enter them. Settlement cannot be requested until they exist (BR-20). They are validated for format only (no live bank check or account-holder name check; documented, not built). Masking: the full account number exists only inside the Payment service (stored encrypted, assumption). Everywhere else (every API response including the claimant's own view and the manager screens, trail entries, events, logs, alerts, ledger, reports) only the last 4 digits are shown. The manager correction screen (BR-49) is write-only: the manager re-types the full number, it is never displayed back. A change of bank details after an earlier submission, or by a manager correction, is flagged "bank details changed" to the checker and kept as a revision (payment-diversion fraud control, industry-typical). Test: settlement request without details returns 422; no response, trail entry, event or log contains a full account number; correction response shows last 4 only.
- **BR-43** (industry-typical principle, assumption on mechanics) Payee. Every payment carries a payee type: `Policyholder` (default), `Nominee` (named on the policy), `ThirdPartyProvider` (e.g. repairer, medical provider: direct settlement) or `Assignee` (assignment of benefits signed by the policyholder). A payee of type Policyholder or Nominee must match the name on the policy (case-insensitive, whitespace and punctuation normalised). Any other payee, or a name mismatch, requires the officer's justification on the settlement request naming the basis (e.g. repairer invoice number, signed assignment) as an Internal entry; the checker sees the payee type, the mismatch flag and the justification before confirming. The documents themselves are not stored in this system (documented, not built). Rationale: paying third parties is normal in motor and property claims, but it is also the main payment-fraud route, so it is allowed with evidence and a second pair of eyes (BR-31). Not built, noted: sanctions and PEP screening, AML/KYC checks on the payee, account-holder name verification, duplicate-account and velocity checks. Test: Policyholder payee with a different name and no justification returns 422; ThirdPartyProvider with justification is accepted and the checker view shows the type and flag.
- **BR-44** (industry-typical) Payment instruction. Created in the same transaction as the manager's settlement confirmation (BR-20), with: paymentId, claimId, payableId, payee type, payee name, bank details (snapshot at confirmation), amount (= payable), currency, idempotency key, status, attempt count. One active instruction per claim (a unique constraint). Test: two concurrent confirms create exactly one instruction.
- **BR-45** (industry-typical) Idempotency and unknown outcomes. The idempotency key is unique per instruction (derived from paymentId and attempt number). A timeout or lost response means the outcome is UNKNOWN, not failed: the payment stays Processing and the Payment service first runs a status check by the same key (and reference) against the gateway. Paid reported: complete as success. Rejected reported: definitive failure, the attempt number advances. Not received reported: the same key and attempt number are re-sent, so the gateway returns the original outcome and the bank is never asked to pay twice. Still unknown after the status-check limit (BR-47): the payment stays Processing, is flagged "outcome unknown" in the manager queue, and cannot be retried or cancelled until reconciliation or the gateway resolves it. Only a definitive failure advances the attempt number. The same key sent twice returns the stored result and creates no second payout, no second ledger entry. Test: send the same key twice, one payout and one set of ledger entries; simulate timeout then success, one payout; timeout with unknown status never creates a second payout.
- **BR-46** (assumption) Payment lifecycle: Pending > Held (funds check failed, BR-67) > Pending/Processing; Pending > Processing > Paid; Processing > Failed (definitive failure only, BR-47); Paid > Reversed; Failed > Processing (retry); Pending/Held/Failed > Cancelled (manager only, never from Processing or Paid, BR-50). Paid is final unless Reversed (bank returned the funds); Reversed and Cancelled are final. A Processing payment with unknown outcome is resolved only by the status check, the gateway or reconciliation, never by a person. Any other transition returns 409. The payout is executed by a SIMULATED bank gateway, a mock that can be told to succeed, fail transiently, fail permanently, or time out per call (for demo and tests). It is not a real bank. Test: each allowed transition works; each other pair is refused.
- **BR-47** (industry-typical principle, assumption on numbers) Failure handling. Failures are classified by the gateway reply. Permanent (invalid or closed account, invalid bank code, account name rejected, blocked by the bank, beneficiary not allowed): never retried automatically; the payment goes straight to Failed after that attempt, with the reason, to the manager exceptions queue, because repeating cannot succeed and risks bank penalties; the manager corrects the payee (BR-49) or cancels. Transient (bank or network unavailable, temporary rejection): automatic retries 3 times with backoff 1 min, 5 min, 30 min, four attempts in total, then Failed and queued. Timeout: not a failure; handled by the status check of BR-45 first, and only a confirmed "not received" or "rejected" counts toward the attempts. Configuration (all configurable, none hard-coded): retry delays, maximum automatic retries, status-check interval and limit (default 3 checks at the retry delays), the failure-code lists for permanent and transient, and a demo profile with seconds-long intervals (e.g. 5 s, 10 s, 20 s). Production would use longer, bank-agreed intervals, and the 1/5/30 minute defaults are an assumption. The claim stays Settling throughout and a manager alert is sent when a payment reaches Failed or "outcome unknown" (BR-65). A funds shortfall (BR-67) is not a failure. Test: gateway set to fail transiently always, exactly 4 attempts then Failed and queued; permanent failure, exactly 1 attempt then Failed; timeout then "not received", same key re-sent, one payout.
- **BR-48** (industry-typical) Settled only on confirmation of payment. The Payment service emits PaymentCompleted (paymentId, claimId, amount, currency, reference, completedAt) once the gateway reports success. Claims consumes it exactly once (processed-event guard) and moves Settling>Settled with actor System, records paid = payable, and writes the trail entry. A claim is never Settled by any other route. Test: duplicate PaymentCompleted changes nothing the second time; a claim with only a Pending/Processing/Failed payment is not Settled.
- **BR-49** (assumption) Manager actions on a Failed payment (Manager only; Officer and Claimant get 403): retry (starts a new cycle of attempts, new attempt number), correct payee details (creates a new revision of the details, entered in full but never displayed back, BR-42, then retry; claimant's original submission is kept as history), or cancel (BR-50). Each creates one Internal trail entry with reason. Test: officer retry returns 403; correction keeps the previous revision.
- **BR-50** (industry-typical principle: a payment can be recalled only before funds leave; assumption on details) Cancel. A manager may cancel a payment only while it is Pending, Held or Failed (no money has left, and for Failed the bank definitively refused it) with a mandatory reason. A Processing payment (including outcome unknown) or a Paid payment can never be cancelled; money that has left is handled only by the bank return of BR-52. On cancel: the payment becomes Cancelled, the payable record is marked Withdrawn, the ledger is reversed by new entries (BR-55 rule 9, original entries untouched, BR-57), reserve is restored to the withdrawn payable amount (BR-22) and the claim returns Settling>Approved so an officer may request settlement again; the next confirmed settlement freezes a fresh payable and a fresh payment instruction (new paymentId and idempotency key), payee details stay on file unless corrected. Cancel is manager-only and needs no second approver because no money moves (assumption). Test: cancel on Processing or Paid returns 409; after cancel exposure rises by the payable amount and the ledger nets to the pre-settlement balances.
- **BR-51** (industry-typical) Metrics. Settlement time = confirmed Approved entry to the Settled entry (BR-29). Also reported: payment success rate, first-attempt success rate, average attempts, count and age of the payment-exceptions queue, time in Settling. Test: settlement time equals the timestamp gap of the two entries.
- **BR-52** (industry-typical principle, assumption on minimal handling) Bank return. If the bank returns a Paid payment (e.g. account closed, found days later), the payment status becomes Reversed, the ledger is reversed (BR-55 rule 8), which reinstates the payable in 2100 so the money is still shown as owed, paid-to-date drops by that amount (BR-72), an Internal entry is written and a manager alert is raised (BR-65). The claim status stays Settled (BR-03) and the returned payment appears in the manager exceptions queue as "returned, payable outstanding" until handled outside the system. Incurred is unchanged. Built: detection, reversal, payable reinstatement, queue item, alert. Documented, not built: re-issuing the payment, reopening the claim for payment only (Settled > Approved with new payee details), claimant notification and request for new bank details, escheat or unclaimed-money handling. Test: Reversed leaves the claim Settled, 2100 holds the payable again, the ledger is balanced and the queue shows the item.
- **BR-53** (industry-typical) Reconciliation. Daily and on demand the Payment service compares its payments with the (simulated) bank statement: each Paid payment must have a matching bank line (key, amount, currency); differences are listed as breaks (missing at bank, amount mismatch, unknown bank line). A second check ties payments to the ledger: ledger cash movements for completed payments equal sum of Paid (Reversed payments being net zero), and the clearing account equals the sum of Processing payments (BR-60). Breaks are reported to the manager; correcting them is by reversing entries only. Test: tamper one amount in the mock statement, the break is reported.

## L. General ledger (module build; double-entry)
Money facts are recorded in a small double-entry ledger owned by a Ledger service, fed only by events (no person posts manually in the first version).
- **BR-54** (industry-typical) Chart of accounts (account code, name, type, normal side), one set per currency (an account is the pair code + currency):
  - 1000 Cash / Bank = the Pool Fund account (asset, debit; BR-66)
  - 1200 Excess recoverable (asset, debit; assumption: reserved for cases where excess is collected from the policyholder instead of netted; unused in the net-of-excess flow)
  - 2000 Claims Reserve (liability, credit)
  - 2100 Payable to claimant (liability, credit)
  - 2200 Payment in transit / clearing (liability, credit; holds money instructed but not yet confirmed by the bank: the obligation to the claimant is not discharged and cash has not left account 1000 until the bank confirms, so it is a liability, not an asset. If a future design debits cash at instruction, the clearing account would become an asset; not our model)
  - 3000 Pool fund contributions (equity, credit; opening balance and top-ups, BR-68)
  - 5000 Claims Paid expense (expense, debit; industry-typical accrual treatment, assumption on timing: paid losses recognised when the payable is raised and the obligation is fixed; cash leaves later through 2100 and 2200)
  - 5100 Change in Claims Reserve expense (expense, debit; movements of the outstanding reserve). Together: incurred loss expense = 5000 Claims Paid + net 5100 Change in reserve; this is the standard incurred = paid + change in outstanding reserve. The simple two-account split is deliberate (decision log 2)
  Balance of a debit-normal account = debits minus credits; of a credit-normal account = credits minus debits. Test: balances computed as stated.
- **BR-55** (industry-typical) Posting rules, one journal entry per business event, amounts in the claim currency; zero-amount movements post nothing:
  1. Claim submitted (reserve = estimate E): Dr 5100 E / Cr 2000 E.
  2. Assessment changes reserve from R0 to R1: if R1 < R0, Dr 2000 (R0-R1) / Cr 5100 (R0-R1); if R1 > R0, the reverse.
  3. Reject confirmed (reserve R released): Dr 2000 R / Cr 5100 R.
  4. Settlement confirmed, payable raised (reserve R, payable P): Dr 2000 R / Cr 5100 R (reserve release) and Dr 5000 P / Cr 2100 P. If R differs from P (should not, BR-22) the difference is first posted as rule 2.
  5. Payment sent: Dr 2100 P / Cr 2200 P.
  6. Payment completed: Dr 2200 P / Cr 1000 P.
  7. Payment failed (attempt): reverses rule 5: Dr 2200 P / Cr 2100 P.
  8. Payment reversed by bank (after rule 6): reverses 6 then 5: Dr 1000 P / Cr 2200 P and Dr 2200 P / Cr 2100 P (the payable is reinstated, BR-52).
  9. Payment cancelled (BR-50): reverses rule 4: Dr 2100 P / Cr 5000 P and Dr 5100 P / Cr 2000 P.
  10. Pool fund opening balance or manager top-up of T: Dr 1000 T / Cr 3000 T (BR-68).
  Payment held for funds posts nothing (no money has moved). Approval and send-backs post nothing (reserve unchanged). Test: each event posts exactly the lines above.
- **BR-56** (industry-typical) Balancing. Every journal entry has at least two lines, each line one account, one side (debit or credit) and a positive amount; total debits equal total credits within the entry, and all lines of an entry share one currency. An unbalanced or mixed-currency entry is refused as a whole and nothing is posted. Test: unbalanced entry returns 422 and persists nothing.
- **BR-57** (industry-typical) Immutable and append-only. Entries and lines can never be updated or deleted. A correction is a new reversing entry (debits and credits swapped) that references the original entry id, with a reason; then the right entry is posted. Test: update/delete attempts fail; reversal leaves the original untouched and nets the pair to zero.
- **BR-58** (industry-typical) Traceability. Each entry carries claimId, paymentId (where the event is a payment event), the source eventId, the claim trail entry id, entryId, postedAt (server UTC), eventType, currency and, for reversals, reversesEntryId. Test: an entry missing claimId, eventId or trail entry id is refused.
- **BR-59** (industry-typical) Idempotent posting. Posting is keyed on the source eventId: the same event delivered twice (Kafka at-least-once) posts once and the second delivery is acknowledged without a new entry. Each payment attempt is its own event, so attempts never collide. Test: replay the same event ten times, one entry.
- **BR-60** (industry-typical) Trial balance and queries. Trial balance lists every account with total debits, total credits and balance, per currency; total debits must equal total credits (the sum of all entries) in each currency, and the report is flagged red if not. Account balance query: by account code and currency, optionally by claimId and as-of timestamp. No cross-currency totals in the ledger; USD only through the reporting conversion of BR-24. Test: trial balance after any sequence of posted events nets to zero per currency.
- **BR-61** (industry-typical) Tie to exposure. Liability exposure (BR-22) in a currency equals the 2000 Claims Reserve balance of that currency summed over open claims; because reserve is released to zero when a claim leaves open status (reject, settlement confirmation), the whole 2000 balance equals open-claim exposure. The "in settlement" figure equals the 2100 plus 2200 balances. Incurred (BR-22 glossary) = 5000 balance + 2000 balance = 5000 + net 5100 (open claim: R; settled: P; rejected: 0). A daily reconciliation job compares the Claims-side reserve with the ledger, and any difference is reported to the manager. Test: reserve per claim equals the 2000 balance for that claim.
- **BR-62** (assumption) Access. Only the Manager role may view the ledger and the pool fund, and top up a fund (BR-69), journal entries, trial balance and account balances; Officers and Claimants get 403; all views are read-only. No person may post, edit or delete entries; corrections arise only from events (BR-55, BR-57). Test: officer GET ledger returns 403.
- **BR-63** (assumption) Periods kept minimal: entries carry postedAt only; there is no period, no close and no cut-off; balances are as-of a timestamp. Documented, not built: period close.

## M. Alerts (claimant and manager)
- **BR-64** (assumption) Claimant alerts for money: "Claim approved: please provide your bank details" (on confirmed approval); "Payment on the way" (when the first attempt is dispatched; once per payment, not per retry); "Paid" with amount, currency and reference (on PaymentCompleted). No alert on retries or failures. Test: three failing attempts produce no claimant alert; success produces exactly one "Paid".
- **BR-65** (assumption) Manager alerts: payment failed after retries (enters exceptions queue); payment reversed by bank (returned, payable outstanding); payment outcome unknown after status checks (BR-45); reconciliation break; ledger out of balance; payment Held for insufficient funds (BR-67); fund below minimum threshold (BR-70). Never sent to the claimant or officer by default. Alerts are idempotent per event. Test: exhausted retries produce one manager alert.

## N. Pool fund (module build, small)
The pool fund is the money from which claims are paid. It is not a claimant wallet.
- **BR-66** (assumption; industry-typical shape: insurers pay claims from a claims disbursement bank account per currency, funded by finance/treasury on a schedule or on cash call; a ring-fenced "pool" per market is typical only of pooled schemes) The pool fund is modelled as that claims disbursement account: one per market and currency (six: SG SGD, HK HKD, MY MYR, TH THB, ID IDR, AU AUD). Fund balance = balance of ledger account 1000 Cash/Bank in that currency; there is no separate stored balance, so there is one source of truth. Funding in real life is a finance routine (e.g. weekly, or when the minimum is breached); here it is the manual manager top-up (BR-68, finance's role is played by the Manager). Opening balances are seeded through a ledger entry (rule 10 of BR-55), illustrative only, not Chubb figures: SGD 5,000,000; HKD 25,000,000; MYR 15,000,000; THB 110,000,000; IDR 50,000,000,000; AUD 5,000,000. Test: fund API balance equals the 1000 account balance after any event sequence.
- **BR-67** (industry-typical) Funds check. Before a payment is dispatched (Pending to Processing) the Payment service checks available funds = fund balance minus payments in transit (2200 balance) in the payment currency. If available is less than the amount, the payment becomes Held with reason "Held - insufficient funds", nothing is posted, the claim stays Settling, the manager is alerted and the payment shows in the manager payment queue (BR-47) marked Held. A Held payment is released automatically (re-checked in order of oldest first) when a top-up or reversal credit is posted, or manually by the manager (re-check, still subject to funds); manager may also cancel (BR-50). A funds shortfall is not a payment failure: it does not consume the 3 retries. Test: payment of 7,500 against available 5,000 is Held and posts nothing; after a 10,000 top-up it is released once; two Held payments are released oldest first and never overdraw.
- **BR-68** (industry-typical) Fund movements, all ledger entries: contribution or top-up (Dr 1000 / Cr 3000); payout (credit to 1000 at payment completed, BR-55 rule 6); reversal (debit to 1000 when the bank returns funds, rule 8); no other movement. Top-ups require a positive amount and a reason, are recorded in the trail of no claim (fund-level audit, with actor from the token) and are idempotent on a request id. Test: top-up posts exactly one balanced entry; a repeated request id posts none.
- **BR-69** (assumption) Who may top up: Manager only. Officers and Claimants get 403 on top-up and on any fund view. A manager may not edit or delete a movement. Test: officer top-up returns 403.
- **BR-70** (assumption) Minimum-balance warning: when a fund's available balance falls below a threshold after a movement, one manager alert is raised (and cleared when it recovers above it). Default threshold 20% of the opening balance, configurable per fund; industry practice would set it from forecast payouts for the funding cycle, which is documented, not built. Test: crossing below the threshold alerts once, not on every payment.
- **BR-71** (industry-typical, revised) Tie to ledger and exposure. Fund balance = account 1000; money owed now = 2100 + 2200 (payables and in transit); exposure per BR-61 = open-claim reserve (2000). Two ratios per currency, manager only, each shown with its figures: payables cover = fund balance / (2100 + 2200), flagged red below 1.0 (this money is due now; assumption on the threshold); reserve cover = fund balance / (2000 + 2100 + 2200), informational and never alerting, because reserves are an estimate of future payments, not an amount due. Not applicable when the denominator is 0. The fund balance never goes below zero: payouts that would overdraw are Held (BR-67). Test: both ratios recompute from ledger balances only; payables 7,500 against fund 5,000 flags red.
- **BR-72** (assumption) Claimants have no wallet, balance or fund view. A claimant sees only, read-only and derived: payable (BR-41, once approved), paid-to-date (sum of Paid minus Reversed on their policy) and remaining policy coverage limit = coverage limit minus paid-to-date, further reduced by frozen unpaid payables of other claims when used for the cap in BR-19. Aggregate-per-policy-period assumption (BR-19). Held for funds, returned payments and failures are never shown to the claimant; their status simply remains Settling (or Settled) and no failure alert is sent. Test: claimant API contains no fund field and no Held reason; a returned payment raises remaining limit back by that amount.
- **BR-73** (assumption) Premium collection into the fund and investment of the fund are documented, not built: contributions are manual manager top-ups only.

## O. Trail corrections, intake and dashboard definitions (answers to the product owner's requests)
- **BR-74** (industry-typical principle, assumption on roles) Correction entry. Type `Correction`, always **Internal**, never Public, so a claimant never sees a correction, its count or a gap. Who: the officer assigned to that claim, or any Manager; Claimant and System cannot (403 plus refused Internal entry per BR-04). The author is accountable and is taken from the token (BR-11); an Officer cannot correct entries on a claim not assigned to them. When: any time, including on Approved, Rejected and Settled claims (audit fixes do not reopen anything). Content: references exactly one earlier entry id on the same claim (a Correction may itself be corrected), mandatory reason (min 10 characters) and the corrected statement (e.g. wrong reason text, wrong amount typed in a summary). A correction changes no status, reserve, paid amount or ledger entry and needs no checker, because it moves no money; if the true value differs from the system of record, the real change is made by the proper command (e.g. assessed amount, BR-21), not by a correction. The original entry is never altered; staff views show it flagged "corrected by <entry>", and metrics (BR-28 to BR-30, BR-40) keep using the original entries and timestamps. If something the claimant already saw was wrong, the fix is a new Public entry produced by the normal event, written by the officer or manager as an ordinary communication; that is documented, not built in Phase 1. Test: officer of another claim gets 403; correction without reason or reference returns 422; claimant API shows no Correction and no changed entry count; original entry unchanged; handling time unchanged after a correction.
- **BR-75** (industry-typical, assumption on actor) "Claim received" entry. It is the single submission entry of BR-04 (not a second one): eventType Submitted, fromStatus null, toStatus Submitted, **Public**. Actor = the authenticated claimant who submitted (actorId and role Claimant from the token, BR-11), because submission is the claimant's act; the wording is system-generated, and role `System` is reserved for machine actions such as PaymentCompleted. If a staff user submits on behalf of a claimant (not built), the actor is that staff user and the summary is unchanged. Content: summary "Claim received: {claimNumber}" using the BR-25 number, and occurredAt = server UTC at commit, shown to the claimant as the receipt time; nothing else (no amount, no assessment). Test: submit creates exactly one entry with actorRole Claimant, visibility Public, summary containing the claim number, occurredAt equal to the claim's submittedAt.
- **BR-76** (assumption, industry-typical principle: FIFO queue by receipt time) "Oldest unassigned" for the manager dashboard. Unassigned = status Submitted with no assignee (the only way into the pick-up queue, BR-26). Age = now minus the claim's submittedAt (the Submitted entry's occurredAt), in calendar time, shown in hours and days; business-hours/SLA clocks are documented, not built. Because no transition ever returns a claim to Submitted (BR-01), reassignment, auto-withdrawal (BR-37) and release (BR-27) keep the claim assigned and never re-enter this queue, so there is no "time since returned" clock. Sort: submittedAt ascending, ties by claim number ascending; the first row is the oldest unassigned. Also shown: count of unassigned claims. Test: claims submitted T1<T2<T3, T1 picked up: oldest unassigned is T2; two with equal submittedAt order by claim number; a reassigned or withdrawn claim never appears.
- **BR-77** (industry-typical, assumption on display) Outstanding-claims count. Outstanding = every non-final claim, i.e. not Rejected and not Settled (same population as open exposure, BR-22): Submitted, UnderReview, InfoRequested, PendingDecisionApproval, Approved, PendingSettlementApproval, Settling. Each claim is counted exactly once, in the group of its own current status; Pending* are never folded into UnderReview or into Approved, and the total is the sum of the groups. Breakdowns by status, market and claim type each add up to the same total; a status x market x type cross-tab is available by drill-down. A convenience group "awaiting checker" = PendingDecisionApproval + PendingSettlementApproval may be displayed as a subtotal, labelled as such, without removing the separate rows. Counts are per claim, never per amount; currency does not matter. Test: with one claim in each of the seven non-final statuses plus one Settled and one Rejected, total = 7 and each status row = 1; sums by market and by type = 7.

## Worked example (one claim, SGD)
Estimate 10,000; excess 500; assessed 8,000; payable = 8,000 - 500 = 7,500 (limit not binding). Illustrative fund for the example: opening 5,000 (the seeded real amount in BR-66 is far larger; small here to show a hold). Entries use BR-55 (accounts: 5000 Claims Paid, 5100 Change in Claims Reserve, clearing 2200 as a liability, decision log 2).
| # | Event | Dr | Cr | Amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Fund opening balance | 1000 | 3000 | 5,000 | Fund 5,000 |
| 1 | Submitted, reserve = estimate | 5100 | 2000 | 10,000 | Reserve 10,000 |
| 2 | Assessment, reserve to 7,500 | 2000 | 5100 | 2,500 | Reserve 7,500 |
| 3 | Settlement confirmed: reserve released and payable raised (one entry, 4 lines) | 2000, 5000 | 5100, 2100 | 7,500 each | Reserve 0, payable 7,500, claim Settling |
| - | Funds check: available 5,000 < 7,500 | - | - | - | Payment Held, manager alerted, nothing posted |
| 4 | Manager top-up | 1000 | 3000 | 10,000 | Fund 15,000; Held payment auto-released |
| 5 | Payment sent | 2100 | 2200 | 7,500 | In transit 7,500 |
| 6 | Payment completed | 2200 | 1000 | 7,500 | Fund 7,500; claim Settled |
Decision approval posts nothing. Final balances: 1000 = 7,500 debit (the fund), 3000 = 15,000 credit, 2000 = 2100 = 2200 = 0, 5100 = 0 net (10,000 - 2,500 - 7,500), 5000 = 7,500 debit. Total debits = total credits = 57,500 (lines: 5,000; 10,000; 2,500; 7,500; 7,500; 10,000; 7,500; 7,500), so the trial balance nets to zero (closing debits 1000 7,500 + 5000 7,500 = 15,000; closing credits 3000 15,000). Incurred = 5000 + net 5100 = 7,500 = paid. Coverage ratio before the top-up: 5,000 / (0 + 7,500 + 0) = 0.67 (flagged); after completion with no open claims it is unbounded and shown as not applicable. The excess 500 never moves because it was netted before payment; account 1200 is unused. Variant, attempt 1 fails then attempt 2 succeeds: entry 5, then reversal Dr 2200 / Cr 2100 7,500, then entry 5 again, then entry 6. Variant, cancelled while Held: reverse entry 3 (Dr 2100 7,500 / Cr 5000 7,500 and Dr 5100 7,500 / Cr 2000 7,500), reserve 7,500 again, claim Approved. Variant, bank returns the payment after entry 6: Dr 1000 7,500 / Cr 2200 7,500, Dr 2200 7,500 / Cr 2100 7,500; payable 7,500 owed again, claim stays Settled, fund 15,000 again. Variant, permanent failure (invalid account) on attempt 1: reversal Dr 2200 / Cr 2100 7,500, payment Failed, no retry, manager corrects payee or cancels. Variant, rejected after the assessment: Dr 2000 7,500 / Cr 5100 7,500; nothing else.
